Natal dispersal across birds

Why do young birds disperse the way they do? A global comparative study of juvenile tracking data.

Status: ongoing since May 2025 · Swiss Ornithological Institute · project page at the Vogelwarte

Natal dispersal, the move from where an animal is born to where it first breeds, shapes gene flow, range expansion and how populations recover. Tracking devices now follow young birds through this journey in dozens of species, yet most studies still analyse one species at a time. This project brings those tracks together to ask which ecological, morphological and phylogenetic traits explain how far, how fast and how directly juvenile birds disperse.

Aims

  • Compile juvenile tracks from many bird species into one dataset with comparable movement metrics.
  • Separate a species’ capacity to move from its dispersal strategy, so large and small species can be compared fairly.
  • Estimate how consistent individuals are in how they disperse, not just how species differ on average.
  • Test the drivers of dispersal with phylogenetic comparative models, and use them to predict where dispersing juveniles go.

Contribute data

If you track juvenile birds, I would like to hear from you.

  • What qualifies: GPS, VHF or Argos tracks of juveniles, in any bird species. We welcome tracks covering any interval from the start of dispersal onwards; they do not need to reach settlement.
  • What I ask for alongside the tracks, if available: natal (birth) location, sex, tag type and model, fix schedule or duty cycle, known fate and first breeding attempt.
  • What contributors get: co-authorship on publications that use your data, regular update emails and access to the live overview.
