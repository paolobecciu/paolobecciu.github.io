**Status:** Scopoli's shearwater, Linosa, 2010–2020, ongoing collaboration; Bermuda petrel project 2023–2024; Galápagos petrel fieldwork 2024

**Scopoli's shearwaters on Linosa.** From my undergraduate years into my PhD I worked on an EU LIFE project on Linosa, monitoring the Scopoli's shearwater (_Calonectris diomedea_) colony while controlling introduced rats and mice. In 2020 I began monitoring the colony with passive acoustic recorders and radar, with the support of [Ornis italica](https://www.ornisitalica.com/). The same colony gave two field experiments: on how artificial light and sound from tourism affect chick weight, and on whether parents recognise their own chick (they adopt foster chicks unconditionally, so probably not).

**Bermuda petrel.** With colleagues in Portugal, Canada and Bermuda I worked on acoustic monitoring and GPS tracking of the Bermuda petrel or cahow (_Pterodroma cahow_), thought extinct since the 17th century and rediscovered in the 1950s. I co-directed, with Letizia Campioni, a 2023–2024 grant from Environment and Climate Change Canada to help increase its population. Tracking showed the species forages mainly at the sea surface and flies more at night.

**White-tailed tropicbird.** Also on Bermuda, citizen-science records of first sightings from 1953 to 2023 point to possible multi-decadal changes in when white-tailed tropicbirds (_Phaethon lepturus_) arrive at their breeding grounds.

**Galápagos petrel.** In 2024, with the [Jocotoco Conservation Foundation](https://www.jocotoco.org/), I carried out fieldwork and GPS tagging of the Critically Endangered Galápagos petrel (_Pterodroma phaeopygia_) at its largest colony, on Floreana Island, to study the species' foraging ecology.

**Methods:** passive acoustic monitoring, marine radar, GPS tracking, citizen-science data.
